Units & Accountability

What each unit does —
and how it's measured.

Every unit head, assistant and general personnel member is scored against the same Balanced Scorecard (BSC) framework. Plain language. No guessing what "good" looks like.

The Framework

Four quadrants. One scorecard.

Every leader's performance is measured across four weighted areas — mirroring how the wider organization measures departmental success.

QuadrantWhat It MeasuresWeighting
Financial / Resource StewardshipHow well a leader stewards the resources entrusted to them.20%
Customer / StakeholderHow well a leader serves attendees, guest ministers and YMR organizers.30%
Internal Business ProcessHow well a leader follows protocol, reports accurately, and responds fast.30%
Learning & GrowthHow well a leader trains, mentors and grows their people.20%

Financial Quadrant — Updated Target

The HOD's Financial / Resource Stewardship target is now 100% — full accountability for every naira entrusted to the department, with no shortfall margin. The Assistant HOD / 2IC target remains at 95%, reflecting the second-tier resourcefulness standard already in place.

Leadership Roles

What HOD, AHOD, Gen Sec & A.Gen Sec are expected to do.

HOD

Head of Department

Owns overall command of the department: budget follow-up, access control oversight, and dissemination of the finalized KPI scorecard to the Board of Trustees. Financial Stewardship target: 100%.

AHOD / 2IC

Assistant HOD

Leads KPI metric scoring and manages the leadership scorecard; supports the leadership scorecard across every unit. Financial Stewardship target: 95%.

Gen Sec

General Secretary

Maintains on-ground command presence during deployments; handles cross-department liaison and official correspondence on behalf of the HOD.

A.Gen Sec

Assistant General Secretary

Handles presentation and slide support, distributes meeting minutes, and pushes final KPI documents to stakeholders after every leadership meeting.

Unit Responsibilities

What every unit is expected to do.

So every personnel member — new or returning — understands exactly what their unit owns.

CI

Central Intelligence

Runs ongoing risk assessments, deploys members to protect VIPs, ministers and delegates, investigates incidents, manages de-escalation and post-event reviews, and coordinates with the Advance Unit on close protection.

Advance

Advance Team

Conducts venue mapping and route security for prayer walks, backs up Access and Ground Control, assists medical teams in emergencies, and ensures pre-event readiness. Mnemonic: "Secure every member."

Access

Access Control

Secures entrances and exits, verifies identification tags and wristbands, and guards against tag misuse or unauthorized passage.

Ground

Ground Control

Ensures auditorium safety for the congregation, prevents theft and disruption, manages lost-and-found, and acts as first point of contact for attendees — escalating incidents when needed.

Market

Market Control

Oversees vendor compliance, prevents unauthorized selling, resolves vendor–customer disputes, and works with the Traffic Unit to manage crowd and vehicle flow near market areas.

Traffic

Traffic

Manages vehicle movement and parking, coordinates with local traffic authorities, and supports Access and Ground Control during peak periods.

Welfare

Management & Welfare

Provides hydration, first aid and basic medical support to volunteers, and manages the daily distribution and retrieval of walkie-talkies and other issued equipment — including gadget accountability (see below).

All Personnel

All Personnel

Paramilitary and military volunteers who patrol and protect security members, prevent harassment of volunteers, and investigate theft, assault and fraud alongside Central Intelligence before handing offenders to the Code of Conduct.

Equipment Policy

Gadget accountability.

Repair-or-Replace Policy

Every walkie-talkie, ID tag and piece of issued equipment is logged to the personnel it's assigned to for that deployment.

  • 01
    If assigned equipment is damaged or spoiled while in a volunteer's care, the Welfare Unit assesses the damage.
  • 02
    If the item can be repaired, the responsible volunteer covers the repair cost before reissue.
  • 03
    If the item is beyond repair, the volunteer pays the current gadget replacement fee before new equipment is issued to them.
  • 04
    The Welfare Unit Head owns day-to-day gadget housekeeping — issuing, logging condition at handover and return, and flagging damage. Unit Heads confirm equipment condition for their own personnel at the start and end of every deployment.
